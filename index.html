The four spheres

Hydrosphere

All of Earth's water: oceans, rivers, lakes, ice and groundwater.

Geosphere

The rocky parts of Earth: crust, mantle, core, mountains and soil.

Atmosphere

The gases that surround Earth, like nitrogen and oxygen.

Biosphere

All living things: plants, animals, fungi and tiny microbes.

Chapter 1

Hydrosphere

  • Water exists in three forms: solid (ice), liquid and gas (water vapor).
  • Oceans hold salt water. Many rivers and lakes hold fresh water.
  • Groundwater is stored below Earth's surface.
  • A reservoir is a place where water collects.

The water cycle

  • Sunlight provides the energy for evaporation.
  • Cooling water vapor condenses into droplets.
  • Precipitation returns water to Earth's surface.
  • Gravity moves water downhill, and the cycle repeats.

Check 1 · Water detective

What is the difference between evaporation and condensation?

Evaporation: liquid → gas. Condensation: gas → liquid.

Why is ocean water salty?

Salts are carried from rocks into oceans, and evaporation leaves the salt behind.

What do we call water stored underground?

Groundwater.

Chapter 2

Geosphere

  • Earth has a crust, upper mantle, lower mantle, outer core and inner core.
  • Crustal plates move slowly.
  • Plate movement can cause earthquakes, mountains and volcanoes.
  • The geosphere changes over time.

Three kinds of rock

Igneous

Forms from cooled magma or lava.

Sedimentary

Material that was deposited and pressed together.

Metamorphic

An existing rock changed by heat and pressure.

The rock cycle has no fixed beginning or end. Any rock can become another kind of rock over a very long time.

Check 2 · Rock challenge

Which type forms from cooling lava?

Igneous rock.

What causes an earthquake?

Energy released when rock breaks along faults causes seismic waves.

Which rock type is changed by heat and pressure?

Metamorphic rock.

Chapter 3

Atmosphere

The air is a mix of gases. Nearly all of it is nitrogen and oxygen.

Nitrogen ~78%Oxygen ~20%
Remaining ~2%: other gases, including carbon dioxide and water vapor
  • Most weather happens in the troposphere, the layer closest to the ground.
  • Air pressure generally decreases as you go higher.
  • The ozone layer in the stratosphere helps protect living things.

Air, pressure and weather

Warm air rises

Cool air sinks. This moving air makes wind and changing weather.

Low pressure

Linked with clouds and precipitation.

High pressure

Generally brings clearer, calmer weather.

Chapter 4

Biosphere

  • The biosphere includes every living organism.
  • Biodiversity means the variety of species.
  • Living things interact with each other and with air, water and rocks.
  • Organisms need resources and energy from their environments.

Chapters 5–8

How the spheres interact

The spheres are not separate. Each one changes the others all the time.

Chapter 5
Hydrosphere interactions
  • Water breaks down rock: weathering.
  • Moving water carries sediments: erosion.
  • Water swaps heat and water vapor with air.
  • Plants and animals use water to survive.
Chapter 6
Geosphere interactions
  • Mountains can change rainfall patterns.
  • Land shape affects where water flows.
  • Aquifers store groundwater in underground rock.
  • Weathered rock and organic material help form soil.
Chapter 7
Atmosphere interactions
  • Wind moves sand and other sediments.
  • Air can change rocks chemically, like rusting.
  • Air and water exchange heat and moisture.
  • Gases in the air support life.
Chapter 8
Biosphere interactions
  • Plants take in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis.
  • Organisms exchange matter with water and air.
  • Roots and burrowing animals can break down rock.
  • Decomposers return nutrients to the environment.
